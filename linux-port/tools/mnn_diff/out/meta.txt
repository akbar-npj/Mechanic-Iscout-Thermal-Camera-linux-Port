## How to reproduce

    cd linux-port/tools/mnn_diff && ./run.sh

With no arguments run.sh regenerates in_plane.raw with gen_plane.c and then
drives the vendor's own mnn_run_2(mnn_mode_name, uint16_t *in, uint16_t *out)
out of libmnnmodel.so (prepared by tools/vendor_shim/prep_vendor_so.py) on this
host, writing vendor_out.raw.  Both files above are reproduced byte-for-byte —
verified with cmp, and the hashes are recorded so a drift shows up immediately.

It is deliberately NOT wired into `make check`: it needs the vendor .so files
from the APK, an aarch64 host with glibc, and the bionic shims.  What `make
check` runs is src/mnn_test.c's differential, which reads the two frozen files
and needs none of that.

vendor.log is a redirect of that run, not something run.sh writes itself:

    ./run.sh > out/vendor.log 2>&1

## The input plane

Synthetic, and deliberately non-constant: a smooth thermal-like field (a
Gaussian blob on a linear gradient), generated by gen_plane.c.

Non-constant is the whole point.  The MNN session tensor is NC4HW4, so a
constant plane produces the same frame whether the samples are written into the
right layout or the wrong one — the differential would pass either way.  See
the layout note in src/mnn_runtime.cpp.

## Expected agreement, measured on this host

src/mnn_test.c asserts that max|delta| <= 1 over all 196608 samples, that at
least 85% are bit-exact, and that the output mean matches the vendor's to
within half a step.

  Measured: 177081 of 196608 bit-exact (90.07%); the other 19527 differ by
  exactly 1, so max|delta| == 1.  Output mean 32874.29 against the vendor's
  32874.38.

The residual is NOT a layout or convention error — those produce a peak error
of 253, and mnn_test fails loudly on them (verified by building the naive
variant).  It is deterministic floating-point drift between the vendor's MNN
2.5.0 and the upstream MNN this port builds against; it is independent of
thread count (numThread 1, 2, 4 and 8 all give bit-identical output) and of
BackendConfig::Precision (Normal and High agree exactly).  Reproducing the
vendor bit-for-bit would mean building MNN 2.5.0 itself, which is not worth it
for a 1-LSB bound.
